On 2026-09-29 the owner reacted to two things I proposed.

**The first plan was textbook ETF trend-following**, tested with a design whose likely outcome was "trade the benchmark". The owner said: "i am not happy ... i asked you to find any idea that might work, think out of the box."

**A research brief with a "quick screen" stage** drew: "quick screen is not good - i want a research backed with facts ... need to search online for trustable resources".

**Why:** the owner judges a plan by whether it can plausibly find something that works. A rigorous test of a low-prior idea reads as effort spent to confirm a null. For new territory, opinion or one-line verdicts aren't acceptable.

**How to apply:**
- When proposing research directions, lead with ideas that differ in kind from what has failed: other return sources, more independent bets, structural payers. Say up front what each can detect.
- Keep the rigour bar. The owner accepted every honest negative result; the objection was to the choice of ideas, not to the rigour.
- For external or web research, require:
  - tiered sources: peer-reviewed or replicated research, then methodical practitioner work, then primary documents for costs, rules and tax;
  - citations with access dates;
  - UNVERIFIED marks where facts can't be confirmed;
  - no marketing or affiliate review sites.
- The owner is open to any broker or service provider, and doesn't want deep dives on the existing brokers' capabilities.
